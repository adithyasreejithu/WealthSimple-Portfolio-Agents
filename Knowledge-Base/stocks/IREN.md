## Company Overview

IREN Limited (formerly Iris Energy) is a vertically integrated data-center operator that builds and runs facilities powered largely by renewable energy. Its legacy business is Bitcoin mining, and it is increasingly redirecting capacity toward AI / high-performance computing ('neocloud') GPU hosting, anchored by a multi-billion-dollar NVIDIA-linked compute contract referenced in recent coverage. The company owns and develops its power and data-center infrastructure to secure a structural low-cost-power advantage over compute peers. It reported roughly $501M of revenue in FY2025 and is US-listed, reporting in USD. The business sits at the intersection of two volatile cycles -- AI-compute demand and Bitcoin -- with heavy ongoing capital expenditure to fund the buildout.

## Original Thesis

IREN owns low-cost, renewable-powered data-center capacity and is converting Bitcoin-mining infrastructure into AI/HPC hosting at a time of acute GPU-compute scarcity. The core thesis is that owned power plus rapid buildout speed plus signed hyperscale/NVIDIA compute contracts let IREN monetize AI demand with structurally cheaper power than pure-play compute peers, turning a cyclical miner into a growth infrastructure business with expanding margins. It is held as a Quality-tilted growth bet on that transition. The principal risks are capital intensity and financing (persistent negative free cash flow), equity dilution, execution on contract conversion, and dual sensitivity to the AI-compute and Bitcoin cycles.

## Updated Thesis

This is IREN's first formal evaluation, so the thesis is being established rather than revised, but the evidence makes the operational case stronger than a skeptic would expect. IREN has converted low-cost, renewable-powered data-center capacity from Bitcoin mining into AI/HPC hosting at a moment of acute GPU scarcity, and the FY2025 numbers validate the pivot: revenue up ~167% to ~$501M, a swing from a -$28.9M loss to +$86.9M net income, ~20.9% margins, and a conservative balance sheet (debt/equity 0.53, current ratio 4.29). Analyst sentiment is buy-skewed with a mean target more than double the current price. On fundamentals alone the thesis is stronger than a typical speculative miner.

What is broken is not the business but the position. At 77.7% of the portfolio the holding is nearly 8x the 10% single-name cap, so the idiosyncratic risks -- deeply negative free cash flow (-$2.31B) from relentless capex, a ~41% one-month drawdown, put-skewed options positioning with a put wall at spot, and sensitivity to both the AI-compute cycle and Bitcoin -- are not diversified away; they are the portfolio's risk. The rubric returns Trim off a middling business composite combined with the floored portfolio_fit, and the sizing makes the current holding untenable.

Net assessment: the underlying investment thesis is intact-to-strengthening on execution, while the position construction is broken. The action that follows is to trim hard toward policy weight, not to add, and to keep a residual position sized to the genuine (but volatile) growth conviction.

## Analyst View

The mechanical rubric lands on Trim, and I agree -- though it reaches the right call partly for the wrong-weight reason. On the business itself, IREN is a genuine growth story executing a credible miner-to-AI-infrastructure pivot: triple-digit revenue growth, a profitability inflection, a conservative balance sheet, and buy-skewed analyst support with a target more than double spot. On a normally sized position I would lean Hold-to-constructive on fundamentals, tempered by the rich valuation, deeply negative free cash flow, and put-skewed options. The single most important fact, however, is that this holding is 77.7% of the portfolio. The portfolio_fit dimension carries only 15% weight and is floored at 1, so a position that is nearly 8x the policy cap only nudges the composite to 2.58 -- enough for Trim here, but the score structurally understates concentration. In portfolio terms this is not a position, it is the portfolio: a leveraged, cash-burning, high-beta AI/crypto name whose 90-day drawdown was ~20% and whose options market is actively pricing downside (put/call 1.4, put wall at spot). Regardless of business quality, prudent risk management demands trimming back toward the 10% cap; the diversification benefit dwarfs any single-name conviction. So my agreement with Trim is driven primarily by sizing rather than by a bearish read on the business. If the rubric were to keep landing on Hold for positions this concentrated (it did not here only because the business composite is middling), the owner should add a concentration override in author-decision-rubric so extreme single-name weight forces a Trim on its own.
